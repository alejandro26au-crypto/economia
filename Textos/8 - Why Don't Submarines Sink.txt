Ever wondered why a submarine floats? 74 tons of steel! It should sink! A schoolteacher cracked it. And the Navy laughed at him!
1875. John Holland sends his design to the US Navy.
Their answer? A fantastic scheme of a civilian landsman. Back to class, teacher!
Everybody knew: steel sinks. Period.
Then something weird happened. Irish rebels paid him to build one. To sink British warships! Then it got weirder. His own backers stole his sub! Nobody could drive it. Then the weirdest part. 1900. The same Navy that laughed paid him 150,000 dollars. America's first Navy submarine!
The teacher was right.
A sub floats when it's lighter than the water it pushes away. Flood the tanks? It dives. Blow them with air? It rises. Holland's trick? Stay 300 pounds light. Dive on the fins. Engine dies? It pops right back up!
The company born from his sub still builds America's subs. 5 billion dollars each. Not bad for a landsman. Now you know.
